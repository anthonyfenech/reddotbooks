OFF-THE-RECORD

by Anthony Fenech

OFF-THE-RECORD is the journey of a gonzo sports writer stuck in a caste society built to control messages and stomp truths. In this modern-day media thriller, the mischevious eye of a misfit baseball writer takes you on a twisted tour of the big leagues, inside the guts of the sports industry, through the decaying newsroom, and onto ESPN's Bottom Line in viral controversy.

OFF-THE-RECORD is all-access, authentic to a fault and brazenly breaks many of journalism's biggest taboos to provide a most unique view of the truth. Branded by journalism's scarlet word, it is what happens when breaking the rules is the only way to survive.

The moment Justin Verlander called him unethical, Anthony Fenech became the story he'd spent five years covering. The Houston Astros backed up the pitcher's claim with three security guards, blocking the Detroit Free Press beat writer from his own clubhouse. Within hours, it was on ESPN's Bottom Line. Within months, it had cost him his beat.

OFF-THE-RECORD is what came before, what came after, and everything in between—five seasons covering the Detroit Tigers from inside the rope line, told from the press box of a newspaper industry collapsing around its own reporters.

Fenech takes readers into the rooms baseball books skip past: the late-night text chains with editors and sources, the hate mail that arrives by the inbox-full, the Cy Young ballots, the pandemic shutdown. A Detroit Free Press beat reporter and Baseball Writers' Association of America member, he writes from the position no baseball book has centered—the reporter asking the questions.

About the author

Anthony Fenech covered the Detroit Tigers for the Detroit Free Press from 2015 through 2020. A member of the Baseball Writers' Association of America, he was twice recognized by the Associated Press Sports Editors. OFF-THE-RECORD is his first book.
